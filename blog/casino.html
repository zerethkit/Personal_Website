Should Singaporeans Visit the Marina Bay Sands Casino?

I learned basic strategy and went to Marina Bay Sands to see how much of it survives a real table.

For my 21st birthday, I decided there was no better way to celebrate than at the Marina Bay Sands casino. Singaporeans pay a $150 levy just to walk through the door, so I was already down even before I started playing.

Here is how they get your money.

I have spent a fair amount of time learning perfect basic strategy for blackjack, so I walked in knowing what to do with every hand. The problem was that the only blackjack on the floor was Free Bet Blackjack. It sounds generous. You can double on 9, 10 and 11 for free, and split most pairs for free, without putting in any more money. The catch is that when the dealer busts on 22, it is a push instead of a win for you. Normal blackjack played by the book has a house edge of about 0.5%. Free Bet Blackjack is about 1.04%, so roughly double. The free doubles are paid for by all the hands you should have won.

The other thing I noticed was that the dealer only draws his second card after everyone has finished hitting. That is the no hole card rule, and it costs you as well. If you have doubled or split and the dealer then turns over a blackjack, the extra money goes with the original bet. It also means the strategy I had memorised was not quite right for that table, because against a dealer 10 or ace you have to stop doubling and splitting into a hand that might already be blackjack.

Overall, I would not spend $150 on the levy again, as the standard games have special rules that make them less favourable, and applied for self-exclusion after that.
